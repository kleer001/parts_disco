Sets the yard could hold

Free model sets that could be the next fleet: things with one shared anatomy and several members you cannot tell apart at a glance. The counts come from the kits' own file lists rather than from their sales copy; every overlap number was measured by running the game's own tracer over the models.

What a set has to be

The board asks one question — which of these is the one I was shown — and a set can only carry it if the members are alike enough to confuse and different enough to separate. The fleet in the game splits three ways, and a candidate set is worth having when it splits the same way:

LOUDcarries its own silhouette; found instantly however crowded the board
PLAINordinary members; the work is searching, not deciding
TWINSone shell, different trim; the work is telling them apart

The tracer sees shape, and nothing else

The views the board draws come out of tools/model_views: an outline, a silhouette and a shaded card, per angle. Colour is not among them. Two things follow, both checked against the packs rather than assumed:

  • A recolour is not a second model. In Kenney's Nature Kit, tree_default and tree_default_dark have byte-identical vertex positions — same shape, different paint, and the tracer has nothing to draw the difference with. Count shapes, not files.
  • How a kit is textured decides how much of it survives. The newer Kenney kits — watercraft, train, cube pets, suburban, blaster, food — put everything on one material named colormap, so nothing of the colour reaches the line art. The older ones (furniture, nature, space, racing) split by material, and the tracer is set to draw material boundaries, so their seams do come through as strokes.
  • A rigged model needs its rig dropped first. Quaternius's animated packs park the mesh under an armature; the tracer's normalise step then throws the evaluated mesh out of frame and every view comes back empty. Stripping the armature modifier and parent fixes it — derig.py beside this page does exactly that, and everything measured below for those packs went through it.

The yardstick

To compare two views: rasterise both silhouettes on one grid and take intersection over union. 1.00 means the board cannot separate them at all; the current fleet lives between 0.64 and 0.86. That band is the thing to judge a candidate against — a set whose pairs all sit at 0.9 is unfair, and one whose pairs all sit at 0.4 has no twins tier.

Every number on this page is the mean over four angles (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°); the game traces eight. Raw per-angle figures are in measurements.tsv.

IoU weighs the silhouette and nothing inside it, so it is the floor of what a player gets rather than the whole of it: a chair and a cushioned chair overlap at 0.94, and at the same angle the tracer draws 20 strokes on one and 26 on the other. A high number means the two are the same size and shape on the board — which is what makes them hard to sort at a glance — not that the drawing is identical.

Nine sets, measured

Ordered by how well the set's own spread matches what the difficulty path needs. Previews are the packs' own art, served from the sites that give the models away.

A hull sits low and wide. Thrown at the same density as cars the board will read as flatter — the throw separation is searched for, so this shows up as density rather than as overlap, and wants a look before the numbers are copied across.

The widest spread of anything measured here, and the tightest twins: a chair and a cushioned chair overlap at 0.94, which is past the sedan and the taxi. Four kitchen chairs on a board painted in one ink is a harder final stage than the game currently has.

What it costs. Chair legs are thin. The tracer simplifies a silhouette at 0.4% of the frame, and at the last stage's size a leg is near that — the hit-test ring may close over the gaps between legs, which makes a click on empty floor between two legs count as a hit. Worth one render at stage-16 size before committing.

Furniture has no natural top and bottom on a board seen from a shallow angle: a table and a stool read as the same plan view. The 0.46 above is the good case, measured at four angles including the two profile ones.

Houses are boxes with roofs. Most of the difference between them is a garage wing, which vanishes when the wing points away from the camera. The 0.61–0.79 band is measured on four angles; the two rear angles are the ones to look at before trusting it.

Four more, unmeasured

Downloaded and counted, not traced. Each is a candidate the measurements above would settle in an afternoon.

Ultimate Gun Pack

worth a look

Forty models from Quaternius, CC0, and the only set found that is already sorted into tiers: the pack render lays them out in rows by class — revolvers, pistols, shotguns, AK-pattern rifles, AR-pattern rifles, SMGs, bolt-action rifles — five or six to a row. Each row looks like a twins tier with the rows far apart, which is a claim the tracer would settle. Kenney's blasters measured 0.53–0.61 on the same shape language, so the within-row pairs here are the number worth taking.

What it costs. It is a rack of guns, which is a different game to a yard of cars whatever the geometry says. That one is not a measurement.

Food Kit

worth a look

Two hundred models from Kenney, CC0, GLB. Not one anatomy but a shelf of small ones: four burgers, three donuts, five sodas, six plates, six cuts of meat, five ice creams. A fleet here is one family per tier rather than one family throughout — burger, cheeseburger, double burger and double cheeseburger is a twins tier that needs no explaining.

What it costs. Two hundred models is a curation job before it is a fleet, and the families are three and four deep where the path wants four abreast.

KayKit City Builder Bits

worth a look

Thirty-two-plus models from Kay Lousberg, CC0, and glTF is in the download. A second, differently-drawn town to set against Kenney's City Kit — the same subject in another hand, which is the cheapest way to find out how much of the suburban result was the subject and how much was the artist.

Ultimate Buildings Pack

not as it stands

Seventy-six models from Quaternius, CC0. Worth looking at precisely because the pack's own render answers the question: the left half is textured and various, the right half is the same buildings untextured, and the untextured half is a row of near-identical boxes. That right half is what the tracer would see.

What it costs. A set whose variety lives in the texture has none for this game. Kept here as the shape of the mistake, so the next promising contact sheet gets the same test: cover the colour and look again.

Where the rest are

Poly Pizza

poly.pizza is the useful surface: it carries Kenney and Quaternius among others, lists a pack's contents model by model with a thumbnail and a licence for each, and hands over a GLB even where the original pack ships FBX and OBJ only. Every Quaternius model measured on this page was pulled from it that way rather than converted. Search it by anatomy — "tang", "sedan", "cabinet" — rather than by pack.

Kenney's 3D shelf

Fifty kits at kenney.nl, all CC0, all shipping GLB, all in the art language the game already speaks. The ones not written up here are mostly modular — walls, tiles, track — which is a different kind of set: pieces that join, not members that differ.

Quaternius

Eighty-three packs at quaternius.com. Nearly all CC0; the Bestiary kit carries a different licence, so it is a per-pack check rather than a blanket one. The older packs ship FBX, OBJ and Blend only, which is what Poly Pizza's GLB mirror is for.

Smithsonian Open Access

Scanned museum objects at 3d.si.edu, released CC0 and downloadable as GLB and OBJ — thousands of them, on the Smithsonian's own account; that figure comes from their release material rather than from a catalogue count, and is the one number on this page not taken from a file. Not a set as such: real objects, one at a time, at scan density rather than game density. Worth knowing about for a fleet that wants to be real things rather than drawn ones, and worth costing carefully — a scan is tens of thousands of triangles where a Kenney boat is hundreds.

How the numbers were got. The Kenney counts come from each kit downloaded and its model files listed; the Quaternius ones from Poly Pizza's model-by-model listing of the same packs. Neither comes from a pack's own description, and where a figure does — the gun pack's forty, the KayKit thirty-two, the Smithsonian's thousands — the text says so. Every IoU comes from running tools/model_views/render_views.py over the model at four angles and comparing the traced silhouettes with silhouette_iou.py, which sits beside this page along with derig.py and the raw measurements.tsv.

Previews are served from the sites that publish the models, and belong to Kenney, Quaternius and Kay Lousberg respectively. Licences were read off each pack's own page on the day this was written; check the page before shipping anything.
