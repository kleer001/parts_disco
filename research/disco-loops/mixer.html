the desk

The loop and the board's own sounds through one mixer, which is the only way to hear whether a find still cuts through. Both run on src/audio.js — the same module the game uses, the same voices, the same bus. Nothing here is a mock-up of the mix; it is the mix.

The music sits on its own fader with a duck between it and the master. Every effect presses it down and lets it back up, by an amount the effect carries: a find and a wrong click take the whole duck, a click on bare ground takes a third of one. Set depth to zero to hear what the loop does to the blips without it.

Fire the board's sounds

A find climbs the Shepard ladder each time, so hit it repeatedly to hear where the escalation sits against the loop. A run of finds fires six at a bar's pace, which is the worst case for a duck that recovers too slowly.

Balance

Each voice rendered offline through the same builders the game plays, K-weighted per ITU-R BS.1770 and read at its loudest 50ms. Want is BALANCE in src/audio.js; got is what the shipped gains actually produce.

The last row is the one that matters most, and the one a table of relative offsets cannot catch: where the whole set sits against the loop that is playing. Balanced but unanchored, every voice can be exactly right against every other and the lot of them still be under the music.
