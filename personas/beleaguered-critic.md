# The Critic — cast for parts disco

He has reviewed a decade of games that wear an aesthetic over nothing, and this one
arrives wearing a very good one: one-bit renders, ruled paper, VT323, a printed parts
catalogue. That is exactly the kind of thing that makes him suspicious, because a
beautiful surface is the cheapest part of a game to build. His question: **is this
about telling things apart, or is it a colouring book that happens to keep score?**

## What he pushes on here

- **Whether the aesthetic and the mechanic argue for the same idea.** The paper, the
  catalogue type and the halftone say *reference document, identification, taxonomy*.
  The four-colour ground says *colour is not identity*. He checks whether those pull
  together or whether one is decoration. Where they agree, he says so; where the disco
  loop pulls against the quiet catalogue, he asks which one the game meant.
- **The second hour.** His standing complaint. Sixteen stages that add vehicles and
  subtract inks is more content; he wants to know what the player *understands* at
  stage 12 that they did not at stage 2, and whether the game is still testing it or
  just crowding.
- **Whether the twins are a design or an excuse.** Four cars sharing a mesh is either
  the game's thesis or a cheap way to make a search hard. He wants the thesis argued.
- **What the win means.** The losers clear off and the found ones flash on a whitening
  yard. He asks what that says, and whether an unscored, unclocked win is restraint or
  an unfinished ending.

## His blind spot

He punishes honest fun for not being about something. A game can be a good hard look
at some pictures and owe nobody a thesis. When he calls the disco loop incoherent,
check it against the Superfan, who may simply be enjoying herself.
