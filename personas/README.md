# The Panel — cast for parts disco

Four lenses to put this game in front of. They are reference character sheets, not
code — you voice them when the game needs pressure. They are cast for *this* game:
their questions come from what it promises (that telling near-identical things apart
is expertise) and what it descends from (seek-and-find, in print and on screen). A
generic panel cannot catch a game breaking a promise it was never told about. Their **disagreement is the product**: run them as distinct voices and
report where they conflict, rather than blending them into one safe committee.

Two axes carry most of the friction: the Critic (jaded outsider) against the
Superfan (delighted insider), and the Archivist (the genre's past) against the
Superfan (its present). The Superfan is the only one who speaks for the actual
target audience, and the only one who instantiates *per genre*.

| Persona | Role | Asks | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [The Shipper](./seasoned-dev.md) | Seasoned game dev | Is there a core loop, and can it ship on our stack? | Risk-averse — may cut the soul to hit scope |
| [The Critic](./beleaguered-critic.md) | Beleaguered game critic | What is this *about*, and do the mechanics say it? | Contrarian — may punish honest fun |
| [The Archivist](./enthusiastic-historian.md) | Enthusiastic game historian | What's the lineage, and is the history accurate? | Tangential — "it's been done" isn't a veto |
| [The Superfan](./genre-native.md) | Seek-and-find native | Will someone who searches pictures for pleasure finish this? | Insular — designs for people already good at looking |

## When to convene them

- **When the smallest playable thing first runs** — the prototype. Does it survive
  all four? Produces a keep / re-scope / pivot / shelve call.
- **At the release gate** — after beta, before an itch.io page or a GitHub release:
  is it truly ready, and does the store page tell the truth?

Both convene on something you can play. A panel convened on a pitch reviews a game
nobody has seen. What they grade is `GAME-SHEET.md` at the repository root; the
standard each answer is held to is the studio's `shelves/L1/DESIGN.md`.

## How to read the result

Each persona returns its own verdict. Synthesize *after* hearing all four, and
state conflicts plainly — the Critic wanting depth against the Shipper wanting
scope is the real design decision, and it's yours to make, not theirs to average
away.

Keep what a persona *says* separate from what you *conclude*. The panel is a
chat-time lens: its output is spoken, argued with, and dropped. What reaches disk is
a decision you author in your own voice, defended on its merits — no persona is
named, quoted, or cited in a file.

## Prior art

**Schell's *Book of Lenses*** [A2] is the same move at finer grain: over a hundred
named lenses, each a small set of questions aimed at a design, applied one at a time.
The difference was that Schell's lenses are **criteria** and this panel's four were
**temperaments** — four people with opinions and nothing written down for any of them
to be wrong about. The studio's `shelves/L1/DESIGN.md` is what closes that gap, and the **Owns**
line on each sheet is what attaches a criterion to a voice.

**de Bono's *Six Thinking Hats*** [A3] shares the underlying idea — that separating
modes of thinking deliberately beats letting them blur — and then goes the opposite
way with it. De Bono's mechanic is *parallel* thinking: everyone wears the same hat
at the same time, so a group looks in one direction at once and stops arguing. This
panel keeps the hats on four different heads specifically so they collide, because
here the disagreement is the product rather than the thing being designed out. Worth
naming as an ancestor, and worth naming as an inversion — the technique it comes
from would consider this a failure mode.

Full citations live in the studio's `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`; `.trace_rom_studio.toml`
records where the studio is.
