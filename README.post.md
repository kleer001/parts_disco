# Twelve cars, and four of them are the same car

parts disco is a browser game about looking. A yard fills with vehicles, drawn as
outlines and flat-coloured so that no two touching shapes wear the same colour. A
panel shows you one of them, solid, at an angle you will not find in the yard. Click
every copy.

That would be a spot-the-difference if the fleet were honest. It is not. Four of the
twelve vehicles are the same car underneath: a sedan, a sports sedan, a taxi and a
police car. Head-on, the only thing between them is a roof sign. So the work is never
seeing the vehicles. It is telling them apart.

Find one and it grows, shakes, flashes green, and settles into a colour it was not
wearing before. That colour is the only record you get. Miss, and a counter goes red.

Sixteen yards. Four things change between them: which vehicles turn up, how many, how
big, and how many colours the yard is allowed. The first matters most by a distance —
a tractor is found instantly however many cars surround it, and a taxi is hard on an
empty yard. The last yard gives you a hundred and twenty small vehicles, four
near-identical cars, and one colour to paint them all. Below five, the yard runs out
of colours, touching shapes start to share, and a vehicle merges into whatever it is
lying on.

This is a prototype, and nobody but me has played it. I do not know whether the
difficulty climbs at the right rate, or whether the near-twins are a good puzzle or a
mean one.

It runs in the browser, no install and no sign-up. If you reach the sixteenth yard, or
give up before it, I would like to hear which.
